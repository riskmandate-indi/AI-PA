## Part 1. What the research says about sound

| # | Finding | Evidence | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Names that are easy to say are trusted and liked more | Alter and Oppenheimer (2006, PNAS). Stocks with easy-to-say names did better at first. The effect faded after about 6 months | Fluency comes first. If people cannot say it on first sight, we lose them |
| 2 | Front vowels (i, e) feel small, light, quick and precise. Back vowels (a, o, u) feel large, warm and solid | Klink (2000). Yorkston and Menon (2004, Journal of Consumer Research) | The best pattern for a calm PA mixes the two. One vowel gives warmth, the other gives precision |
| 3 | Soft consonants (m, l, n, r) feel round and gentle. Hard stops (k, t, p) feel sharp and spiky | Bouba/kiki research. Consonants carry more of this effect than vowels (Fort and Schwartz 2022). Tested across many languages (Ćwiek et al. 2022) | "Calm" and "care" are brand values. Lean on m, l, n, r. Use k and t sparingly |
| 4 | Words whose consonants move from the lips towards the throat are liked more. Example: BODIKA is preferred over KODIBA | The "in-out effect" (Topolinski and colleagues). Many replications. The cause is still debated | A small effect. Used here only to break ties |
| 5 | Systematic review: high-frequency sounds tie to positive evaluation and lower potency | 2022 systematic review (Oxford). The evidence base is mostly about taste | Supports a light front vowel for a friendly feel |

My own working rules, drawn from the evidence above:
- English favours strong-weak stress (TA-da). Every candidate stresses the first syllable.
- A name that ends in an open vowel (-a, -i) is easier to say aloud and to call out.
- "sh" is a calming sound (think of "hush"). "k" adds energy but also edge.

### Scoring rubric (out of 10)

| Code | Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F | Fluency: say and spell it on first sight | Hard | Some doubt | Effortless |
| S | Softness: calm sound | Mostly hard stops | Mixed | Mostly soft sounds |
| R | Rhythm: 2 syllables, stressed first, flows | Awkward | OK | Smooth |
| D | Distinctiveness: sticks in memory | Very common word or name | Fairly common | Unusual but easy |
| C | Clean: no negative meaning, religious weight or brand clash | Serious issue | Minor issue | Clear |

## Part 5. Recommendation

- **Keep Darshi as the lead.** Round 2 confirms it on sound as well as meaning.
- **Melos is the best new find.** Choose it if the brand should lead with feeling, not function. Tagline idea: "Life, in tune."
- **New top 3: Darshi, Melos, Kavi.** Nitya drops out because of "nit". Memor drops to 4th because of the "Memorii" UK filing.
